# Present Perfect Simple vs. Continuous // B2 EXAM TELEMETRY

### 1. CORE CONCEPT & USAGE

**The Distinction:**
The choice between the **Present Perfect Simple** and the **Present Perfect Continuous** hinges on the speaker's focus: **Result/Completion** versus **Duration/Activity**.

*   **Present Perfect Simple (`have/has + past participle`)**
    *   **Usage:** Emphasises the completion of an action, the result achieved, or a specific number of times. It is used with stative verbs (know, like, believe) and achievements.
    *   **Focus:** The outcome matters more than the process.
    *   **Syntax:** `Subject + have/has + V3`

*   **Present Perfect Continuous (`have/has + been + present participle`)**
    *   **Usage:** Emphasises the duration of an activity, often with visible evidence or physical signs resulting from the action. It implies the action may still be happening or has just stopped.
    *   **Focus:** The process matters more than the result.
    *   **Syntax:** `Subject + have/has + been + V-ing`

**Key Syntax Formulas:**

| Tense | Formula | Time Markers (Common) | Contextual Nuance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple** | `have/has + [V3]` | *just, already, yet, ever, never* | Completed action; result is visible. |
| **Continuous** | `have/has + been + V-ing` | *for, since, all day, lately, recently* | Unfinished activity; physical evidence (tiredness). |

### 2. GOLDEN RULES & EXAM TRAPS

*   **Stative Verb Prohibition:** Never use the Continuous form with stative verbs (e.g., *know, understand, believe, like, own*).
    *   *Trap:* `I have been knowing him for years.` ❌ -> `I have known him for years.` ✅
*   **The "Result vs. Process" Trap:** If a physical result is highlighted, use Simple; if the activity itself or temporary state is highlighted, use Continuous.
    *   *Trap:* `You look tired.` -> `Have you been working?` (Process) vs. `I have finished the work.` (Result).
*   **Time Markers:** Words like *for* and *since* do not automatically dictate the tense.
    *   *Trap:* `I have lived here for 5 years.` (Still living there) vs. `I have lived here for 5 years and moved out last week.` (Simple implies completion).
*   **Irregular Verb Forms:** Ensure the Past Participle is correct in Simple forms, as errors here are heavily penalised in Use of English Part 4.
    *   *Trap:* `I have went` ❌ -> `I have gone`.

### 3. USE OF ENGLISH PART 4 TRANSFORMATION PATTERNS

In Cambridge B2 First (FCE) Part 4, candidates must rewrite sentences maintaining meaning while altering structure. The following patterns dictate the tense choice during transformations.

| Original Structure | Transformation Keyword/Logic | Target Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `I have been waiting for an hour.` | **Duration + Ongoing** | `How long have you waited?` (Simple) OR `I have been waiting...` (Continuous) |
| `She has finished her homework.` | **Completion/Result** | `She hasn't started yet.` / `Her homework is done.` |
| `He has lost his keys.` | **Completed Action with Result** | `He can't find his keys because he has lost them.` |

**Conversion Logic for Part 4:**
1.  **Duration Focus:** If the transformation requires a question about *how long*, use Present Perfect Simple (`have + V3`) if the action is viewed as a completed block of time, or Continuous (`have been + V-ing`) if the activity implies continuity up to now.
2.  **Stative Conversion:** If the sentence contains a verb like *know* or *believe*, force the transformation into Present Perfect Simple regardless of duration markers.
3.  **Evidence Focus:** If the context implies physical evidence (e.g., dirty clothes, red eyes), shift from Simple to Continuous (`have been + V-ing`).

### 4. HIGH-SCORING EXAMPLES

1.  "I've just been commuting by train since I moved here; it's actually quite a relief compared to driving in this traffic."
    *(Focus: Process/Activity over time)*

2.  "She has completed the project ahead of schedule, which means she hasn't had to work overtime for weeks."
    *(Focus: Result/Completion)*

3.  "We've been trying to contact the client all morning, but their line keeps cutting out."
    *(Focus: Ongoing activity with current relevance/evidence)*
