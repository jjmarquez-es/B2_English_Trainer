## 🗣️ 2. Common Phrasal Verbs & Collocations

These are high-frequency expressions used with the vocabulary above to sound more natural and advanced (C1).

### Economic & Business
*   **Run into red tape:** To encounter bureaucratic obstacles. (*"The project was delayed because we ran into red tape."*)
*   **Drive up prices:** To cause inflation or increase costs. (*"Supply chain disruption drove up prices."*)
*   **Attract investment:** To bring in money from investors. (*"Tax incentives attract investment.")*
*   **Foster an ecosystem:** To encourage growth of a specific environment (business/startups). (*"The city fosters an entrepreneurial ecosystem.")*

### Environmental & Urban Planning
*   **Offset carbon:** To compensate for emissions. (*"We need to offset carbon through planting trees."*)
*   **Combat sprawl:** To stop uncontrolled urban expansion. (*"Zoning laws combat sprawl.")*
*   **Transition energy:** To switch from fossil fuels to renewables. (*"The government is transitioning energy sources.")*
*   **Reduce footprint:** To lower environmental impact. (*"We must reduce our ecological footprint."*)

### Social & Legal
*   **Uphold rights:** To protect and maintain rights. (*"The court upheld civil liberties.")*
*   **Marginalise communities:** To push groups to the edge of society (negative). (*"Poverty can marginalise communities.")*
*   **Promote inclusion:** To encourage participation of all groups. (*"Social inclusion initiatives promote inclusion.")*
*   **Zero tolerance:** A policy where no exceptions are made for a rule. (*"The school has a zero tolerance approach to bullying.")*

### Traffic & Infrastructure
*   **Ease congestion:** To reduce traffic jams. (*"New lanes ease congestion.")*
*   **Seamless interchange:** Moving between transport modes without delay. (*"The station offers seamless interchanges.")*
*   **Park and ride:** A scheme where you park outside a city center to take public transport. (*"Use the park and ride scheme to save money.")*

## 💡 Tutor's Tip for B2/C1 Learners

**Context is King.** Notice how words like *subsidised*, *resilience*, or *plurality* change meaning slightly depending on whether you are talking about **economics**, **environment**, or **human rights**.
*   *Example:* "Resilience" in economics means bouncing back from a crash. In ecology, it means an ecosystem recovering from damage.
*   *Tip:* When writing essays or speaking formally, always pair these terms with their specific collocations (e.g., don't just say "market," say "flourishing market").

Keep practicing by reading news articles about **urban planning** and **economic policy**. Good luck with your studies!
